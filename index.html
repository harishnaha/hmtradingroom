🛡️ Golden Rule
Profit is the reward. Discipline is the requirement.

Never increase position size just because a target is close. A target is achieved only when the profit is made while your risk rules and trading process remain intact.

Mindset Principles The rules that protect your capital, process and decision-making.
TRADER'S MINDSET

Think like a risk manager. Trade like a business owner.

Good trading is not about being right every time. It is about controlling risk, following a repeatable process, and staying emotionally stable long enough for your edge to work.

“
Protect the downside first. Capital is the inventory of your trading business.
01

Cut Your Losses Early

A small planned loss is a business expense. A large uncontrolled loss can damage both capital and confidence.

Set the stop before entry. Never move it farther away just to avoid taking a loss.
02

Protect Your Capital

Your first job is survival. Position size should be small enough that one trade, one day, or one bad sequence cannot seriously damage the account.

Risk a fixed, predefined amount per trade and reduce size when conditions are poor.
03

Treat Trading Like a Business

Think in terms of capital, risk, operating rules, performance review and long-term expectancy—not excitement or quick money.

Every trade should have a reason, risk limit, execution plan and review.
04

Start a Trading Journal

Record the setup, entry, exit, risk, result, market context and your emotional state. A journal turns experience into measurable information.

Review patterns weekly instead of judging yourself from one trade.
05

Process Over Outcome

A good trade can lose money, and a bad trade can make money. Judge yourself primarily on whether you followed your process.

Grade execution separately from P&L.
06

Be Okay With Waiting

There is no requirement to trade every market move. Waiting for your highest-quality setup is an active trading decision.

No setup = no trade. Avoid forcing opportunities.
07

Losses Are Part of the Game

Losses are unavoidable even with a profitable strategy. The objective is not zero losses—it is keeping losses controlled while allowing winners to pay for them.

Accept the planned loss without revenge trading or increasing size emotionally.
08

Cup With Handle Breakout

A continuation setup where price forms a rounded consolidation (the cup), followed by a smaller pullback or tight consolidation (the handle), then breaks above resistance.

  • Prefer a clear prior uptrend.
  • Look for a rounded base rather than a sharp V.
  • Handle should normally be tighter and shallower than the cup.
  • Breakout should clear the handle resistance with convincing participation.
  • Define invalidation below the structure before entry.
09

Trendline Breakout / Trend Continuation

Use a clearly defined trendline to identify structure and potential continuation or change in momentum. A trendline is a guide, not a signal by itself.

  • Use multiple meaningful swing points.
  • Wait for price confirmation instead of trading every touch.
  • Combine with trend, support/resistance, volume or another independent confirmation.
  • Enter only when the risk-to-reward profile is acceptable.
10

Confluence Before Entry

The strongest trades often have several independent reasons supporting the same idea. Confluence should improve selectivity—not become an excuse to justify a trade.

  • Trend / market structure
  • Support or resistance
  • Volume or momentum confirmation
  • Price-action setup
  • Clear invalidation and sensible risk/reward
11

Never Chase

If price has already moved far beyond your planned entry, the original risk/reward may no longer exist.

Missed trade > bad trade. Wait for the next valid setup.
12

Stop After Your Daily Risk Limit

After a predefined daily loss limit is reached, step away. Continuing to trade while frustrated often turns a manageable day into a damaging one.

Protect tomorrow's capital by respecting today's limit.

Before Every Trade

A quick mental checklist

DISCIPLINE CHECK
✓ Is this my planned setup?
✓ Is the market structure clear?
✓ Where is my invalidation?
✓ How much am I risking?
✓ Is the reward worth the risk?
✓ Am I calm enough to execute?
✓ Am I entering because of a signal—not FOMO?
✓ If this loses, will I accept it without revenge trading?
Core principle: Consistency comes from repeating good decisions, not from predicting every market move.

Data Safety & Backup

Your journal is stored in the browser's IndexedDB. When PIN Lock is enabled, the journal database is encrypted with AES-GCM. Use the backup buttons below to keep a copy outside the browser. You can also select a dedicated folder for the database backup.

Recommended backup routine

1. After important changes, click Backup Full Journal (JSON).
2. Save the downloaded file somewhere outside the browser profile.
3. Keep at least 2–3 dated backup copies.
4. To recover, use Restore Full Journal.

JSON preserves trades, Pre-Market Plans, Reflections, Goals, Models, Checklists and account settings. Exported JSON backups are not encrypted, so store them securely. CSV is mainly for trade data and Excel analysis.

Storage: IndexedDB · Database: TradingJournalProDB · localStorage is used only for small compatibility metadata

📱 iPhone / iPad Backup & Restore

For iPhone, use the full JSON backup. It is the safest way to move your complete journal between devices.

Backup on iPhone: tap Backup Full Journal → Share sheet → Save to Files or choose OneDrive.
Restore on iPhone: tap Restore Full Journal → choose your saved trading-journal-backup-*.json file from Files/OneDrive.
Important: JSON backup contains the complete journal data. Keep 2–3 dated copies.
